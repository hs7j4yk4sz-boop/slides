FAIR PLAY belongs to a family, and we say so. 1926: Alexander Calder builds his Circus in Paris, a miniature fair he performs himself; it is now at the Whitney Museum. 1959: Jean Tinguely shows his Méta-Matics at Galerie Iris Clert in Paris, machines that draw; visitors make about 4,000 drawings. 1961: Niki de Saint Phalle turns the shooting gallery into painting with her Tirs. 1987: Luna Luna, in Hamburg, by André Heller, the first art amusement park, with a carousel by Keith Haring, a Ferris wheel by Jean-Michel Basquiat and a pavilion by Salvador Dalí; restored and shown in Los Angeles in 2023 and New York in 2024. Every ride of ours has an ancestor: the photobooth and the Surrealists’ Photomaton portraits (1929), the shooting gallery and Niki de Saint Phalle, the arcade and the video games collected by MoMA since 2012. Sources: Whitney Museum; Wikipedia (Métamatic, Luna Luna); Smithsonian Magazine.

Same four beats on every ride. Pay: tap a card or a phone, no app, no account. Play: sixty to ninety seconds, a camera watches you. Generate: AI turns the player’s photo and score into a piece in the artist’s style. Win: everyone gets a print, the target score gives an edition, the best score of the day a 1/1. You pay for an artwork, never for a chance: that is the rule of the fair.

Three machines any artist can take, each with a small screen on its side that says what the AI does. Photobooth: pull the curtain, sit down, your portrait comes out in the artist’s world; the artist brings 10 to 20 works and a print frame, no code, no game. Arcade: a cabinet in your colours, a 90-second game with your name on it, and the score shapes the artwork; bring a game on our template, or the art and we pair you with a developer. Claw: a real crane machine, people fish for a print that exists only once; bring your works, a few editions and one unique piece. The machines are built by hand, in plywood. The objects shown are concept models, not built yet, and the screens are illustrations.

Who does what. You bring the world: 10 to 20 works that define your style, the art for your booth and your print frame, the prizes on your own ride (a small edition and a 1/1), and for the arcade a game or its art. On your own ride you set the price of a play, and you can sell your works at your stand. We bring the machine: built and tested, a wooden booth in your colours, contactless payment and colour printing, the software, the staff and the crowd. The ask is files, not hardware.

Our partners fund the fair · artists only cover the costs

Three ways in.

Three ways in: a shared machine for artists, your own stand for artists and galleries, and the fair at NFC Summit for one brand. The partners of FAIR PLAY fund the fair; what an artist pays is only the cost of the package: the place, the machine, the passes. Every way in comes with a sponsor level of NFC Summit and its perks.
